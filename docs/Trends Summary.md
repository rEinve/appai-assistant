# Trends Summary

Current market research and industry benchmarks highlight a rapid transition toward agentic commerce—where AI agents discover, evaluate, and execute purchases on behalf of consumers.

1. Market Sizing & Explosive Growth Trajectory

Trillion-Dollar Opportunity: McKinsey projects that global consumer commerce mediated by AI agents will reach $3 trillion to $5 trillion by 2030, with up to $1 trillion in the US B2C retail market alone. Bain & Company estimates that agentic shopping will account for 15% to 25% of all US e-commerce sales by 2030, while PayPal/Edgar Dunn & Company forecasts a $1.7 trillion market by 2030.

2026 as the Breakout Year: 2026 is widely recognized as the breakout year as all major AI ecosystem leaders—Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, and Perplexity—have launched live agentic shopping programs.

High Adoption Rate: Over 45% to 50% of online consumers report already using AI tools at some stage in their purchasing journey, and 52% of large enterprise merchants report integrating agentic commerce into regular operations.

2. Higher Conversions vs. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Trust Gap

3–5× Higher Conversion Uplift: Guided agentic shopping experiences convert 3–5 times better than traditional search-and-browse e-commerce because AI agents eliminate decision friction by filtering products against precise buyer constraints.

The Execution Trust Gap: While ~65% of shoppers trust AI to research, compare prices, and evaluate products, only 8% to 14% currently trust AI to complete payments fully autonomously without human review. As a result, most current live implementations still utilize a "human-in-the-loop" model, where the agent handles discovery, cart creation, and option ranking, but routes to the user for final purchase approval.

3. Convergence Around Open Protocols

To prevent fragmented integrations, the industry is standardizing around key open protocols:

Commerce Protocols: Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) (co-developed by Google and Shopify) covers the end-to-end shopping journey across AI surfaces, while OpenAI and Stripe’s Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) focuses on product discovery and native checkout.

Data Connectivity: Anthropic’s Model Context Protocol (MCP) connects AI agents directly to real-time merchant product catalogs, inventory, and pricing feeds.

Payment & Intent Authorization: Standards such as Google’s Agent Payments Protocol (AP2), Visa’s Trusted Agent Protocol (TAP), and Mastercard Agent Pay establish cryptographic identity, verifiable user intent, and tokenized spend mandates.

4. PayPal's Strategic Infrastructure & Capabilities

As AI shopping accelerates, PayPal is positioning itself as a core trust and payment layer across multiple AI surfaces:

PayPal Agent Ready: A payment solution that allows existing PayPal merchants to accept agent-initiated payments on conversational or browser-automated AI channels without new technical integrations, embedding PayPal's fraud detection, buyer protection, and dispute resolution into AI purchases.

PayPal Store Sync: Connects merchant product catalogs and inventory across leading AI platforms (including ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, and Microsoft Copilot) through a single integration while keeping the merchant as the merchant of record.

Developer & Onchain Ecosystem: The open-source PayPal Agent Toolkit exposes REST APIs as LLM tool calls for automated order creation, invoicing, and dispute management. Additionally, PYUSD (PayPal's regulated stablecoin) acts as a machine-to-machine settlement asset across multi-chain networks.

5. Security Concerns & The Shift to "Know Your Agent" (KYA)

Top Barrier to Investment: PayPal’s 2026 Agentic Commerce Pulse Report shows that data security and privacy are the #1 barrier for 94%–99% of large enterprises considering agentic commerce, driven by fears of corporate data leaks and PII exposure.

Evolving Risk Stack: Because automated agents produce different session and device signals than human buyers, traditional risk tools struggle. Merchants and payment networks are adopting Know Your Agent (KYA) frameworks to cryptographically verify agent identities, enforce programmatic spending limits, and protect against AI-driven carding or credential-stuffing attacks.
